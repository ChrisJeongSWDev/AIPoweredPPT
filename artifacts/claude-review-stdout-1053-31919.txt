## What the inputs actually show

The most important discovery is that **the team already has a slide DSL and doesn't know it.** All three HTML files independently use the same convention: `<section class="card" data-slide="N">` is one slide, with a consistent block taxonomy — `<h2>` title, lead `<p>`, `.alert-box` key-message callout, `<h3>`+`<ul>` bullets, `<table>`, `.mermaid` diagram, `<pre><code>`, and `<img>`. The Matrox manual is 17 slides, the 3D Logic design doc is 9 slides with **3 images embedded as base64 data URIs**, and `Slide Template.html` is a 4-slide reference that even has a "PPT 발표 모드" presentation mode and the Vieworks red `#DB0032` baked in as an accent variable.

That reframes the brief's question #2. It is not "HTML-to-PPT *or* python-pptx" — those are different layers. The right answer is **HTML → JSON IR → python-pptx**, where an intermediate representation is the contract:

- **The AI never emits `.pptx` or geometry.** LLMs cannot reliably write DrawingML, and any HTML→PPT path that renders through a browser produces flat screenshot images — no editable text, no native tables, and the master template becomes decoration. That directly violates "the master slide must be the core template."
- **The AI emits IR** (schema-constrained JSON: pick a `layout_id`, fill placeholders by index). That's validatable and retryable.
- **Deterministic Python renders IR → PPTX** against the template's *own* layouts. Design quality becomes a property of code and the template, not of model luck — which is the only way to get "intuitive, precise, clean" reliably.
- HTML then becomes a *second input adapter* into the same IR (ingest the 26 slides of existing decks) and a free preview renderer. Nothing is wasted.

The biggest quality risk I want the main worker to confront head-on is that **python-pptx has no autofit** — it will not shrink text that overflows a placeholder, and it silently produces ugly slides. That, not the AI, is what will make output look bad.
